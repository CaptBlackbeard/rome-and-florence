Rome & Florence

SEVEN DAYS · APRIL 2027

The whole thing, start to finish.

Two cities, ninety minutes apart by train. Four nights in Rome, three in Florence, and an April that is warm enough to sit outside at night but early enough that the lines at the Colosseum are still short.

Everything below is real: the flights we would book, the hotels with their actual nightly rates, the trattorias, the gelaterias, the wine bars with the cheese boards. It is planned around a kid five and under, which mostly means gardens, gelato, and never more than one big thing a day.

The weekOne headline thing each day, and nothing else scheduled on purpose.

Day 1

Out of LAX, overnight

An evening departure, dinner somewhere over the Atlantic, and we wake up in Europe. Premium economy means the seats recline properly and a small one has room on a lap or a spare seat.

Day 2

Land in Rome. Do almost nothing.

Check in, drop the bags, and give the day away to the neighbourhood: a walk with no destination, an early dinner, an early night. Jet lag with a toddler is won on the first afternoon or not at all.

With a small one: a piazza, a fountain to throw pebbles in, and pizza by 6pm.

Day 3

The Colosseum, first thing

We go in between 8:30 and 10am, before the tour groups stack up and while the light is still low and gold across the arches. The ticket also covers the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill next door, so the rest of the morning can go wherever we feel like.

Tickets come from the official park site: parcocolosseo.it.

One thing worth knowing. Villa Borghese is not walking distance from the Colosseum — it is about two miles, 45 to 60 minutes by bus and metro. So it gets its own half-day instead of being bolted onto this one. The gardens themselves are free; only the Galleria Borghese needs a separate timed reservation.

Day 4

The Vatican, then dinner in Trastevere

Museums in the morning. Tickets open exactly 60 days ahead and the skip-the-line option is about five euros on top of the twenty euro base — the easiest five euros on the whole trip. museivaticani.va

Then we cross the river for the evening. Trastevere is cobblestones, ivy, lanterns strung between buildings, and the best concentration of old-school trattorias in Rome. The list of exactly where to eat is further down.

With a small one: the museums are stroller-friendly; Trastevere at dusk is a toddler's idea of a party.

Day 5

Rome to Florence by high-speed train

City centre to city centre in roughly 90 minutes, at 300 km/h, with actual legroom and a table. No airport, no security line, no car seat. Afternoon: check in, then wander Piazza della Repubblica while the light goes long.

With a small one: a very fast red train, and then a carousel in the piazza that runs into the evening.

Day 6, morning

Boboli Gardens

Terraces, fountains, grottoes, hedged paths and a lot of room to let a toddler simply run. Tickets bundle with the Bardini Gardens and are booked through the official Uffizi site — uffizi.it. Last admission is an hour before closing, so we go in the morning.

Day 6, afternoon

The Duomo and the Ponte Vecchio

The cathedral is five minutes' walk from the bridge, so this is one easy loop rather than two errands. We time the Ponte Vecchio for late afternoon, when the jewellers' shutters are still open and the Arno turns the colour of the buildings.

With a small one: you can see the whole dome from the piazza without climbing a single one of its 463 steps.

Day 7

Home

Out of Florence, or back through Rome, depending on how the flights land when the booking window opens. Either way: one last coffee standing at a bar.

Where we eatPizza, cacio e pepe, gelato made on site, and the wine bars with the good cheese. Every rated place links to its Tripadvisor page.

Rome — Monti, by the Colosseum

Alle Carrette 4.0

Thin, crisp Roman pizza about ten minutes' walk from the Colosseum. Loud, cheap, fast, fine with kids. Vicolo delle Carrette 14

Fatamorgana Monti 4.5

Inventive flavours, all natural, with a proper gluten-free range. Walkable from the Forum. Piazza degli Zingari 5

Gelateria S.M. Maggiore 4.7

A neighbourhood gelateria that makes everything on site — the highest-rated stop on this whole list. Via dello Statuto 18

Ciuri Ciuri

Sicilian: cannoli filled to order, granita, and pistachio gelato that tastes like pistachios. Via Leonina 18

Rome — Trastevere, for the Day 4 dinner

Da Enzo al 29 4.3

The one everybody means when they say “a trattoria in Trastevere”. Family-run, tiny, no reservations — go at opening or expect to queue. Via dei Vascellari 29

La Tavernaccia da Bruno 4.4

Fifty years in one family, a wood-burning oven, and a wine list well above what the room suggests. Book this one. Via Giovanni da Castel Bolognese 63

Osteria da Zi Umberto 4.0

Tables out on the cobbles and the tonnarelli cacio e pepe people come back for. Piazza di San Giovanni della Malva 14

Otello

Traditional Roman, quieter than the rest of the street, reservations recommended. Via della Pelliccia 47/53

Rome — wine, and what comes with it

Il Goccetto 4.3

Wood panelling, an 18th-century painted ceiling, hundreds of labels on the wall and a serious cheese and salumi counter. Near Campo de' Fiori. Via dei Banchi Vecchi 14

Enoteca Cul de Sac 4.1

Just off Piazza Navona, a narrow railway-carriage of a room, famous for its cheese and pate boards. Piazza Pasquino 73

Il Covino

Small Prati enoteca near the Vatican that pairs its wines with actual plates of pasta, not just snacks. Via Fabio Massimo 37

Del Frate

The Prati neighbourhood standby — long list, calm room, easy after a morning in the museums. Via degli Scipioni 118

Fortunato al Pantheon & Enoteca Il Piccolo

Two more central classics if we end up walking the Pantheon and Navona end of town.

Florence — Piazza della Repubblica, Day 5

Badiani since 1932

The buontalenti — egg-yolk, cream and sugar, invented in Florence — is the reason to go. Viale dei Mille 20

Rinascente rooftop food floor

Better gelato than anything directly on the piazza, plus a terrace looking straight at the Duomo. Piazza della Repubblica 1

Florence — Duomo and Oltrarno, Day 6

Gustapizza 4.4

Proper Neapolitan bases, heart-shaped if you ask, five minutes from the Ponte Vecchio. Get there early. Via Maggio 46

Berberè

Slow-risen sourdough pizza cut into eight shareable slices — the easiest dinner of the trip with a toddler. Via dei Benci 7

Le Volpi e L'Uva 4.6

Tiny, just over the Ponte Vecchio, small-producer wines by the glass and the best cheese and crostini boards in the city. This is the one I would plan the afternoon around.

Enoteca Alessi 4.5

Fifty years in business, a block from the Duomo, cellar downstairs. Via dell'Oche 27r

Casa del Vino

Behind the San Lorenzo market: wood-panelled, standing room, crostini and a glass of Chianti at lunchtime. Via dell'Ariento 16r

Il Santino

A six-table wine bar in the Oltrarno, the little sibling of Il Santo Bevitore. Via di Santo Spirito 60r

There is a true nonstop on ITA Airways, LAX straight to Rome. We are not taking it: the Iberia routing is premium economy for the long leg, with two bags a person and seats together, and with a small child along that is worth more than the connection is worth avoiding. Exact takeoff and landing times get pinned when the booking window opens — airlines do not publish April 2027 schedules yet.
