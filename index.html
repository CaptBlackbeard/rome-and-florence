Rome & Florence

SEVEN DAYS · APRIL 2027

The whole thing, start to finish.

Two cities, ninety minutes apart by train. Four nights in Rome, three in Florence, and an April that is warm enough to sit outside at night but early enough that the lines at the Colosseum are still short.

Everything below is real: the flights we would book, the hotels with their actual nightly rates, the trattorias, the gelaterias, the wine bars with the cheese boards. It is planned around a kid five and under, which mostly means gardens, gelato, and never more than one big thing a day.

The weekOne headline thing each day, and nothing else scheduled on purpose.

Day 1

Out of LAX, overnight

An evening departure, dinner somewhere over the Atlantic, and we wake up in Europe. Premium economy means the seats recline properly and a small one has room on a lap or a spare seat.

Day 2

Land in Rome. Do almost nothing.

Check in, drop the bags, and give the day away to the neighbourhood: a walk with no destination, an early dinner, an early night. Jet lag with a toddler is won on the first afternoon or not at all.

With a small one: a piazza, a fountain to throw pebbles in, and pizza by 6pm.

Day 3

The Colosseum, first thing

We go in between 8:30 and 10am, before the tour groups stack up and while the light is still low and gold across the arches. The ticket also covers the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill next door, so the rest of the morning can go wherever we feel like.

Tickets come from the official park site: ticketing.colosseo.it.

One thing worth knowing. Villa Borghese is not walking distance from the Colosseum — it is about two miles, 45 to 60 minutes by bus and metro. So it gets its own half-day instead of being bolted onto this one. The gardens themselves are free; only the Galleria Borghese needs a separate timed reservation.

Day 4

The Vatican, then dinner in Trastevere

Museums in the morning. Tickets open exactly 60 days ahead and the skip-the-line option is about five euros on top of the twenty euro base — the easiest five euros on the whole trip. museivaticani.va

Then we cross the river for the evening. Trastevere is cobblestones, ivy, lanterns strung between buildings, and the best concentration of old-school trattorias in Rome. The list of exactly where to eat is further down.

With a small one: the museums are stroller-friendly; Trastevere at dusk is a toddler's idea of a party.

Day 5

Rome to Florence by high-speed train

City centre to city centre in roughly 90 minutes, at 300 km/h, with actual legroom and a table. No airport, no security line, no car seat. Afternoon: check in, then wander Piazza della Repubblica while the light goes long.

With a small one: a very fast red train, and then a carousel in the piazza that runs into the evening.

Day 6, morning

Boboli Gardens

Terraces, fountains, grottoes, hedged paths and a lot of room to let a toddler simply run. Book Boboli itself on uffizi.it. The combined Boboli and Bardini ticket is €15, but it is sold only at the ticket office, on the day — you cannot buy that one online in advance. Last admission is an hour before closing, so we go in the morning.

Day 6, afternoon

The Duomo and the Ponte Vecchio

The cathedral is five minutes' walk from the bridge, so this is one easy loop rather than two errands. We time the Ponte Vecchio for late afternoon, when the jewellers' shutters are still open and the Arno turns the colour of the buildings.

With a small one: you can see the whole dome from the piazza without climbing a single one of its 463 steps.

Day 7

Home

Out of Florence, or back through Rome, depending on how the flights land when the booking window opens. Either way: one last coffee standing at a bar.

Start checking weekly from around the middle of October 2026. Missing trains on a date that is already open usually means they have not been loaded yet, not that they are cancelled.

Which fare, with a small child

FrecciaFAMILY is the buried win. For two adults and one child in standard class, a live quote came back at €50.00 in total — barely more than two adult Super Economy tickets on their own, and unlike Super Economy it can be changed and refunded. It has to be bought by midnight fifteen days before departure.

Super Economy is the cheapest, and the most brutal. No changes, no refund, and you cannot take a different train. For €3 a ticket, Trenitalia's tiRimborso add-on makes it refundable up to midnight two days before, minus 10% — cheap insurance when you are committing six months ahead with a toddler.

Children ride free, but check the threshold. On Trenitalia a child under four travels free on a lap with no ticket at all, or at half price if you want them to have their own seat. Italo's cut-off is tighter: 36 months. A two-and-a-half-year-old clears both, but only just on Italo, so count the months against the actual travel date. Pushchairs travel free in the luggage spaces on Trenitalia.

What the blogs get wrong

You do not validate a high-speed ticket. The green and yellow validating machines on the platforms are for paper regional tickets. Frecciarossa and Italo tickets are ticketless with an assigned seat, and Italo says plainly that no check-in is needed. Plenty of travel advice repeats the validation warning indiscriminately — it does not apply to this journey.

Your seat is assigned and the ticket is good for that train only. You cannot simply hop on an earlier one.

If you miss it, the fare you bought decides everything. On a Base ticket you can take another train of the same type from an hour before to an hour after, if you speak to staff before boarding and pay €10 a head. On Economy or Super Economy you are simply travelling without a ticket. Italo sells an hour of grace for a flat €10 plus any fare difference.

At the station

Both stations are entirely step-free — level access between every platform, the concourse and the street, with no ticket check on the way out. With a pushchair that matters more than anything else here.

Arrive twenty to thirty minutes early. Platforms are often not posted until shortly before departure, so stay near the boards; you can get ahead of them on RFI's live departures monitor, which unlike Trenitalia's own app also covers Italo. Left luggage is on platform 24 at Termini and down platform 16 at Santa Maria Novella, both roughly 7am to 9pm, around €10 to €12 a bag per day. Taxi ranks sit directly in front of both stations. Termini is a known pickpocket spot; keep the bags closed and in front of you on the concourse.

Book direct. The ticket costs the same wherever you buy it — the difference is fees and which trains you can see. Trenitalia's site and ItaliaRail show only Trenitalia; Trainline and RailEurope show both operators, which is the real reason to open them. Omio may add a service fee.

Every price above is a snapshot taken on 25 September 2026 for travel dates up to March 2027, so treat them as a planning anchor rather than a quote. The Base fare sat unmoved at €50 across every date sampled; Super Economy swung between €23.90 and €35.90. Re-check once April 2027 actually goes on sale. No live Italo fare could be read — their booking engine blocks automated access — so Italo's numbers here are its own published figures, not observed ones.
